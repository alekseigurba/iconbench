# 1.0.0

The first release: a bench for drawing minimalistic SVG icons for
[domain-map](https://github.com/alekseigurba/domain-map). Solid lines, fills and
layers on a 24×24 canvas, kept in packs that share one palette, in a library
that is a folder on disk or a Docker volume.

It takes what made sense from domain-map — the tokens and the chrome, the layer
control, the colour picker, the Catmull-Rom maths, the file store — and the same
approach: pure HTML and JavaScript, no build step, no framework, and here no
dependencies at all.

Everything below started as a bullet in the backlog. This page says what each one
became, and why it became that.

## How it works

### Chrome

- The **header** holds the logo, the one heading on the page, and straight after
  it the **File** menu — everything that is about files rather than about the
  drawing. The plan asked for Open and Save; it grew a rule and a second half
  once packs arrived, and *Open* became the pack panel's list.
- The **toolbox** is a rail fixed on the left of the stage and never folds away:
  what the pointer does is the first thing to know about the canvas. The tool in
  hand is filled rather than outlined, since down a column of identical squares
  a border alone does not say which one you are on.
- The **panel on the right** is Spline, then Fill, then the colour both share: a
  picker that is always open, and under it the palette.
- **Poppins**, and domain-map's tokens cut down to the ones this page uses, so
  the two tools read as one family.
- The design system is written down in [design-system.md](../../design-system.md),
  with `app/css/tokens.css` as the source of truth.

### Canvas

- **24×24**, the size Material, Lucide, Feather, Tabler, Heroicons and Remix draw
  on, with a dashed **live area** 2px in on every side that leaves 20×20 for the
  artwork.
- The 24 is the icon's coordinate system, not the size it is worked at: the
  artboard is zoomed to fill the stage, so a pixel of the icon is some thirty
  pixels of screen. Drawing large and scaling down to publish was considered and
  turned down — it turns a 2px line and a half-pixel corner into whatever they
  happen to divide to, and what is on the canvas would no longer be what ships.
- Clicked points land on half pixels, which keeps a 1px or 2px line crisp either
  way; Alt places one freely, and a free-hand stroke is never snapped.
- The icon is shown beside the canvas at **16, 24 and 48 pixels**, which is the
  only honest test of whether it reads. This was not in the plan; it cost fifteen
  lines, since the preview is written by the same code that saves the file.

### The sketch layer

- A guide to draw over, under the icon's layers: **paste a picture** with Ctrl+V
  or the toolbox button, and draw on it with the **marker**. It can be dimmed,
  hidden and cleared.
- It is kept out of every saved file by not being in the document at all. It
  lives beside it, in the tab, and survives a refresh — so "exclude the sketch
  from export" is not a rule the exporter has to remember.
- An SVG that was not drawn here has no lines to edit, so **Load from…** puts it
  on the sketch, where a picture to trace belongs.

### Layers

- domain-map's control, bottom left: one row per layer, topmost first, reading
  `name · drawing on · dim · eye`. There the layers are a fixed model; here they
  are the icon's own, so they can be added, renamed in place and deleted.
- **Only the layer being drawn on answers the pointer.** That is what makes the
  choice mean something beyond where the next line goes, and what makes a dimmed
  layer safe to draw over.
- **Hidden is saved, dimmed is not.** A hidden layer is left out of the picture
  the file shows, written `display="none"`, and is there to be shown again when
  the icon is next opened. Dimming is a way of looking at the canvas.

### Lines

- Three kinds. A **straight line** turns a corner at every click. A **quadratic
  Bézier** takes its clicks in turns — an anchor, then the control that bends the
  run to the next anchor — which is `Q cx cy x y` drawn the way it is written. A
  **Catmull-Rom curve** runs through every click and finds its own way between
  them; it is written to the file as the cubic Béziers an SVG path can hold.
- Click the first point to close a line, double-click or Enter to finish it
  open, Backspace to take back the last click, Esc to give the line up.
- A finished line is selected, so the panel is already about it, and the next
  click starts another.
- With the select tool: drag a line to move it, drag a bend to reshape it,
  Shift+click to add a bend, double-click one to remove it. A quadratic is split
  exactly, so the line does not move under the pointer; taking an anchor out of
  one joins the two runs that met there, and the anchor stays on as the control
  of the joined run, so the curve keeps leaning the way it did.
- Undo and redo are whole copies of the icon rather than domain-map's inverse
  steps: an icon is a few dozen lines, and a copy cannot get out of step with
  itself. A drag or a slider pull is one step.

### Free-hand

- For a pencil, a finger or the mouse. **On release the stroke becomes an
  ordinary line**, with bends to drag like any other — there is no separate kind
  of thing called a free-hand stroke in a file.
- **Smoothing** is how much of the stroke is kept. *No smoothing* joins every
  sample straight. *Min*, *normal* and *max* thin the samples — dropping a point
  if it lies within 0.1, 0.25 or 0.5px of the line drawn without it — and run a
  Catmull-Rom curve through what is left.
- A stroke let go within a pixel of where it began is a closed shape, since that
  is what a hand drawing a circle means.
- Two fingers pan and zoom, and a pencil is never one of them, so a palm on the
  glass does not draw.

### Line and fill

- Per line: **width**, **line colour and opacity**, **closed or open**, and
  **fill colour and opacity**. With nothing selected the panel sets what the next
  line will wear — what was last chosen is what the pen holds.
- The palette is **32 colours, eight across**: domain-map's 24, with a column of
  blacks and a column of greys in front, white among them for knocking one shape
  out of another.

### Save and load

- The library is files: `npm start` keeps it in `storage/`, `docker compose up`
  in the `iconbench-data` volume, and `STORAGE_DIR` points it anywhere else. No
  database and no accounts — it is one person's bench.
- An icon is saved **as the SVG it is**, so the library is a folder of icons any
  page can use rather than a folder of project files. What the editor needs to
  open one again — layers, kinds, points, swatches — rides in `data-*`
  attributes that nothing else reads.
- **Save to…** writes the icon to a file anywhere; **Load from…** brings an
  iconbench SVG in from anywhere and adds it to the open pack.
- A stored SVG that carries script is refused, by the page and again by the
  server, and every stored file is served under a policy that lets it be a
  picture and nothing else.
- Unsaved work survives a refresh, and the page asks before a tab holding any is
  closed.

### Icon packs

- An icon lives in a **pack**: a folder, `packs/<pack>/`, of icons with a
  `pack.json` beside them holding the palette they share.
- The **panel on the left** lists the pack's icons, each with its drawing, to
  pick one for the canvas. **Preview pack** shows them all on one sheet — the
  only way to see whether they belong together.
- A line wears each colour in one of two ways. Pressed in the palette, it is worn
  **as a swatch**: the line keeps the swatch's number and follows the palette
  from then on. Made with the picker, it is **the line's own** and follows
  nothing. The file holds the hex either way, because a file has to draw without
  a palette to ask.
- **Edit palette** changes a swatch, and with it every line wearing that swatch
  in every icon of the pack — the open icon as you drag, the rest of the pack's
  files when the editor is shut. Only the files that wear what changed are
  rewritten.
- Recolouring a pack is deliberately not something the panel's picker can do. It
  writes to every file in the folder, so it happens in a dialog that says so.
- **Save pack to…** and **Load pack from…** carry a whole pack, palette and
  icons, as one `.iconpack.json` — for moving between two machines that each
  keep a library of their own.

## Known gaps

- Layers cannot be reordered. Lines can, within their layer, with `]` and `[`.
- An SVG drawn elsewhere cannot be edited, only traced.
- A pack cannot be renamed or deleted from the page; it is a folder, and can be
  from there.
- Palette edits are not undone by Ctrl+Z. The palette is the pack's and outlives
  the icon's history; **Reset to defaults** is the way back.
