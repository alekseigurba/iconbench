# 1.1.0

The first release drawn with. Seven things from the backlog, most of them what an
afternoon of drawing real icons showed was in the way: a line that started too
heavy, a palette that offered thirty-two colours and no opinion, a sketch that
followed you from icon to icon.

The file format is the one it was, and an icon saved by 1.0 opens. The stock
palette is new, though, and what that means for a pack already saved is said
under it.

## How it works

### A palette of families

- The stock palette is still **32 swatches, eight across** — but 1.0's grid was
  domain-map's fills with two columns of greys in front, a box of colours. Now
  **each column is a family**, and reads downwards the way it is used: a colour
  to draw lines in, then three tints of it to fill with — medium, light,
  lightest.
- The eight line colours are **grey** `#282828`, **burnt orange** `#c4561e` and
  domain-map's **brand green** `#527a42`, then on round the wheel from the green:
  **teal** `#2a7f78`, **blue** `#2f6690`, **purple** `#6b4fa0`, **red** `#b3362d`
  and **ochre** `#a5790f`. They are muted, to sit together, and every one is dark
  enough to hold as a fine line on paper.
- The tints are 30, 70 and 85% of the way to white, which is how domain-map's own
  brand scale steps — the green column *is* its brand 400, 300, 200 and 100, and
  the grey keeps its charcoals.
- Eight line colours is more than any one set of icons should wear. It is what
  lets each set pick its own two or three and find their fills under them.
- There is **no pure white or black**. domain-map shows icons on coloured ovals,
  where a white fill would be a white patch rather than a knock-out; either is
  one typed hex in the picker, as a line's own colour.
- **A new line wears swatch 1**, the grey. A **fill switched on for the first
  time** is the light tint in the column the line's colour is in, so the two
  belong together without being chosen; a line in a colour of its own takes the
  grey column's.
- **A pack already saved keeps the palette it has.** Its `pack.json` holds its
  own copy, and nothing changes until **Reset to defaults** is pressed in the
  palette editor — which now puts *these* colours in. A line keeps its swatch by
  number, so that press recolours every line in the pack wearing one: a 1.0 line
  on swatch 17 goes from the old dark grey to the new light one. That is the
  pack's palette doing what it is for, but it is worth knowing before pressing.
- The first cut of this release added the scheme as twelve extra swatches in a
  block of their own, above the grid. Two palettes in one panel was one too
  many, and it went before the release did.

### The palette above the picker

- In the panel's colour section the **palette now comes first and the picker
  under it** — one grid, with what each row is for on every swatch's tooltip. A
  swatch is what is reached for most, and it is the one that keeps a pack
  recolourable; the picker is for the colour that is a line's own.
- The picker is still always open. Folding it away would have cost a tap for
  every custom colour, and the panel has the height for both.

### A finer line

- A new line is **0.75 wide**, down from 2, and the scale runs **0.25 to 4** in
  quarter steps, down from 8. On a 24px canvas a minimal icon is mostly air, and
  a 2px line is a bold one.
- 0.75 sits on the slider's quarter steps, which sit on the half-pixel grid.
- A line 1.0 drew wider than 4 is **held to 4** when its icon is opened, rather
  than costing the icon: a value off the scale goes to its nearer end.

### A sketch for every icon

- The sketch was the tab's: one guide, under whichever icon was on the canvas.
  It is now **the icon's**. Leave an icon and its sketch leaves with it; come
  back and it is there as it was; a new icon starts with none.
- It is still **never in a file**, and still the tab's to keep: the sketches of
  the icons that are not on the canvas wait on a shelf in the tab's session
  storage, by pack and icon. A refresh keeps them, closing the tab drops them,
  and drawing on one never marks its icon unsaved.
- When the storage fills it is pictures that go first — the shelf's, then the
  canvas's — before any marker line or any of the icon itself.
- A sketch follows its icon: through **Save as**, where the new icon and the old
  both keep it, since both were drawn over it; through a pack's rename; and out,
  when the icon or its pack is deleted.

### Layers, reordered

- **Up** and **down** in the head of the layer control move the layer being
  drawn on one place in the pile. What is higher paints over what is lower, on
  the canvas and in the file. Each move is a step to undo.
- Buttons rather than dragging: the list is 170px wide, and a row dragged with a
  pencil is a row dropped in the wrong place.

### Show and hide the grid

- The **#** button that now heads the zoom stack, or **G**, hides the pixel grid
  and shows it again. The browser remembers.
- Only the grid goes. The live area and the centre lines are the brief — 20×20,
  and even about the middle — and the icon bare of everything is what the
  preview beside the canvas is for.

### Rename and delete a pack

- **Rename pack…** and **Delete pack…** join the File menu. Renaming keeps the
  icons, the palette and whatever is unsaved on the canvas: it is the folder
  that moves, not the work. A name already taken is refused.
- Both are done over the store's four calls — get, put, delete, list — rather
  than a new one, so the store stays something S3 could stand in for. A rename
  copies every file under the new name first and only then takes the old ones
  away, so one cut short halfway leaves two packs, never half of one.
- Only the library's own files move: a pack's icons and its `pack.json`.
  Anything else put in the folder by hand is left be, as the server leaves it.
- **Deleting asks once**, naming the pack and how many icons go with it, and
  cannot be undone: the library is not under version control unless you put it
  there. The next pack opens, or an empty `my-icons` if that was the last.
- The store now drops a folder when the last file in it goes, which is what
  makes deleting a pack's last file delete the pack.

## Known gaps

- An SVG drawn elsewhere cannot be edited, only traced.
- A sketch does not outlive its tab, and does not travel between machines.
- Palette edits are not undone by Ctrl+Z. The palette is the pack's and outlives
  the icon's history; **Reset to defaults** is the way back.
